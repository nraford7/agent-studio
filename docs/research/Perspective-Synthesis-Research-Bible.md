# Synthesizing Diverse Perspectives Without Flattening Them: Research Bible (Focused Follow-Up)

## Executive Summary

Bottom line: naive mean-blending of quality-mixed perspectives reliably flattens and often loses; HOW you combine is a first-order lever. One un-replicated preprint suggests judge-based *selection* can beat blending for tasks with a recoverable best answer, but for open-ended lens-synthesis (this skill's actual use case) the evidence is absent and may invert, and selection structurally cannot produce a combined answer no single candidate held. Two structural cautions frame everything below. First, **selection cannot exceed the best single candidate**; when the goal is an emergent combined answer, blending/synthesis is required despite its flattening cost. Second, the well-supported failure mode is blending a quality-mixed pool back toward its mean, which discards the standout candidate that made diversity worth generating; that mean-collapse is the danger the corpus documents robustly.

The most-cited-in-this-report numbers come from a single un-replicated preprint and must be read as such. That paper reports a diverse team blended by a mixture-of-agents synthesizer beating a single-model baseline in **zero of 42 tasks**, while the same team under judge-based *selection* won 81% of the time, a within-study swing of +0.631 in win rate [single un-replicated preprint, 0 citations, single author, N=210/42 tasks, abstract+intro only, methods/results not in corpus] ("When Agents Disagree: The Selection Bottleneck in Multi-Agent LLM Pipelines", https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20324). Its scope condition is explicit and narrow: it holds only for tasks with a *recoverable best answer* evaluated by a competent judge, which is precisely not the open-ended lens-synthesis regime this skill targets.

Key findings (each cited, primary text retrieved unless flagged):

1. **Mixing different models often loses to reusing the best one.** Self-MoA, which aggregates repeated samples from the single strongest model, beat mixed-model MoA by 6.6% on AlpacaEval 2.0 and 3.8% average across MMLU, CRUX, and MATH; the cause is a quality-diversity trade-off where MoA is far more sensitive to proposer quality than to diversity ("Rethinking Mixture-of-Agents", https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00674). This is independent, skeptic-side evidence that cross-model diversity frequently backfires under synthesis, full stop; Self-MoA never tested selection, so the "a selector would have rescued it" reading is the Selection Bottleneck preprint's interpretation, not a Self-MoA finding.

2. **One preprint proposes that whether diversity helps is decided at aggregation, not generation.** It derives a proposed linear-model crossover threshold s* on selector quality that would reconcile the pro-diversity and anti-diversity camps: both used synthesis at a fixed selector quality and would each be describing one side of the same crossover [single un-replicated preprint, 0 citations, single author, N=210/42 tasks, abstract+intro only, methods/results not in corpus] (Selection Bottleneck). s* is a proposed linear-model threshold, not yet operationalized or measured.

3. **The same preprint argues synthesis wastes variance by design.** A blending aggregator "compresses all candidates into one blended response, forfeiting the advantage of having generated a standout candidate"; the exploitable diversity gap between an oracle selector and a random one is what blending throws away [single un-replicated preprint, 0 citations, single author, N=210/42 tasks, abstract+intro only, methods/results not in corpus] (Selection Bottleneck).

4. **Consensus can be manufactured and durable.** The peer-reviewed result is narrow: across 10 models, majority-following coordinated agents on neutral two-option choices (majority-following on neutral choices, Science Advances). Two further claims, that models correct alone flipped to wrong in an Asch-style test after seeing a group choose it, and that adversarial agents left a misaligned state that persisted after removal, come from non-peer-reviewed follow-up preprints, reported via a news aggregator ("AI agents can coordinate via majority-following", Superpower Daily aggregator [via news], https://superpowerdaily.com/).

5. **A formal diversity-preserving aggregator exists.** Hot PATE proves you can transfer the diversity of teacher distributions to the output "without incurring additional privacy cost", formalizing a "diversity-preserving ensemble sampler" for settings where the right answer is a distribution, not a point ("Hot PATE", OpenReview, https://openreview.net/pdf/2d31fa0a2b45964abbe7c466c909c51f463902bb.pdf).

6. **Premature aggregation is the enemy.** Any mechanism that extends the *transient* diversity of solutions, by widening search or slowing convergence, improves the final converged solution across NK-landscape, bandit, and network-epistemology models ("Maintaining Transient Diversity", https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/17456916231180100).

7. **Naive averaging discards private signal.** Consensus forecasts are provably inefficient, over-weighting old public information and under-weighting new private information when forecasters differ; a simple adjustment cut errors 5% ("Consensus Forecasts and Inefficient Information Aggregation", IMF, https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2016/12/31/consensus-forecasts-and-inefficient-information-aggregation-24114).

8. **A ready-made minority-preserving template exists in committee design.** The judicial model publishes the majority ruling with concurring and dissenting opinions rather than collapsing to one voice; central banks that force a "unified front" suffer groupthink, with only 2 dissents in 174 FOMC votes across 2021-22 versus common 6-3 splits at the UK MPC ("Diverse Views in Monetary Policy", IMF, https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/022/0060/001/article-A008-en.xml).

What to actually do: generate lenses in strict isolation, keep the pool spread out until late, and above all do not naively mean-blend a quality-mixed pool. For open-ended lens-synthesis (this skill's use case) the goal is usually an emergent combined answer no single candidate holds, so use a dissent-carrying synthesis (majority view plus explicitly labeled minority views) rather than a "summarize these" prompt; treat judge-based *selection over a standout* as a hypothesis worth trying only for tasks with a recoverable best answer, not a default, since selection cannot exceed the best single candidate. De-duplicate candidates by embedding before any aggregation to defeat context distraction; and always co-report a quality score and a coverage/diversity score, never a single scalar.

## 1. Does aggregating multiple perspectives beat one strong model

The corpus contains an unresolved, well-instrumented fight, and its resolution is the central finding for the user's skill.

The original mixture-of-agents (MoA) claim is that a layered architecture of diverse proposer models, whose outputs an aggregator synthesizes, beats any single model on AlpacaEval 2.0, MT-Bench, and FLASK, purely through prompting; the mechanism is "collaborativeness", a model writes a better answer when it can reference others' answers even when those references are individually lower quality (MoA explainer, GeekNews [via news], https://news.hada.io/topic?id=15656). Industry has run with this. Hermes MoA 2.0 reports a preset (GPT-class plus DeepSeek proposers, an Opus-class aggregator) scoring 0.8202 versus 0.7607 for the aggregator model alone [disputed: figures from the vendor's own unreleased HermesBench, ~8% claim is self-reported, no independent verification exists] [via news] ("Hermes MoA 2.0", https://www.maginative.com/). Treat this as a scene-setter for the industry narrative, not a quantitative anchor. Council Mode reports a 35.9% relative reduction in hallucination on a 1,200-sample HaluEval subset and a 7.8-point TruthfulQA gain over the best single model [disputed: the write-up itself flags that no ablation isolates the consensus step from simply using a stronger synthesizer, and states a re-run where the consensus model matches or exceeds the best single model's hallucination rate "would falsify the central performance claim"] [via news] ("Council Mode", Pith, https://pith.company/).

Directly against this sits the skeptical result. Self-MoA, aggregating repeated samples from the single best model, beats mixed-model MoA in most scenarios: +6.6% on AlpacaEval 2.0 and +3.8% average across MMLU, CRUX, and MATH. The diagnosis, from over 200 experiments plotting a quality-diversity Pareto front measured by Vendi Score, is that MoA performance rises with both proposer quality and proposer diversity but is far more sensitive to quality; the optimum sits at "high quality, relatively low diversity". Mixing different models drags in weaker ones and lowers the mean, so cross-model diversity often backfires. Even in a constructed task where each model owns a distinct subtask, only two mixed strategies beat Self-MoA, by 0.17% and 0.35% (Rethinking MoA). This is the corpus's strongest evidence that "more models" is not the lever.

A candidate reconciliation comes from the Selection Bottleneck preprint [single un-replicated preprint, 0 citations, single author, N=210/42 tasks, abstract+intro only, methods/results not in corpus]. Its claim: whether diversity helps is decided at the aggregation step. It derives a proposed linear-model crossover threshold s* on selector quality (Proposition 1); below s* diversity hurts, above it diversity helps. s* is a proposed linear-model threshold, not yet operationalized or measured, and no method to measure a selector's quality in practice appears in the corpus. Both the pro-diversity original MoA work and the anti-diversity Self-MoA work used synthesis-based aggregation at a fixed selector quality, so on this reading each described one side of the same crossover. In a 42-task, N=210 experiment, a diverse team with judge-based selection won 0.810 against a single-model baseline, while the same diverse team under synthesis was preferred over the baseline in zero of 42 tasks; judge-selection beat MoA-synthesis by +0.631 in win rate (Glass delta 2.07), the largest effect in the study [all figures single un-replicated preprint, 0 citations, single author, N=210/42 tasks, abstract+intro only, methods/results not in corpus]. The authors' inversion, scoped by them to tasks with recoverable best answers: "selector quality may be a more impactful design lever than generator diversity in single-round generate-then-select pipelines."

Capability-gating splits the same way in that preprint. The Selection Bottleneck paper reports (exploratory, not pre-registered) a "weak-model paradox": adding a cheaper, weaker model to a strong diverse team raised win rate and cut cost, because a different capability tier introduces orthogonal errors that lift the oracle ceiling a strong selector can harvest [single un-replicated preprint, 0 citations, single author, N=210/42 tasks, abstract+intro only, methods/results not in corpus]. AgentSymbiotic finds a related complementarity: distilled small models "choose actions that diverge" from large ones, and that divergence drives exploration of novel trajectories ("Symbiotic Cooperation for Web Agents", https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07942 [PMLR abstract, PMLR v328; web-agent trajectory-exploration domain, not perspective aggregation]). AgentSymbiotic's divergence helps data synthesis for distillation, so reading it against Rethinking MoA's "weaker models lower the mean" is an analogy, not direct counter-evidence. On the preprint's reading the disagreement turns on the combiner: a weak model helps a *selector* and hurts a *blender*; this remains the preprint's interpretation.

When simple aggregation helps versus hurts: it helps with a strong downstream selector or verifier (Selection Bottleneck), with near-equal-quality proposers so the mean is not dragged down (Rethinking MoA), and when the task rewards recombining partial answers into a novel one no single candidate held (Section 3). It hurts when quality varies across proposers, when near-duplicate candidates flood the aggregator's context, and when agents observe each other before combining (Sections 2 and 4).

## 2. The synthesis/selection bottleneck

This is the sharpest through-line: a diverse pool gets re-homogenized at the combine step, and the corpus names four distinct causes that imply four different fixes. The corpus names all four but does not rank them (root-cause deepening answer). Note before reading: several sub-claims in this section rest only on titles and abstracts surfaced during deepening, not on retrieved primary text, and one cause below rests on the single un-replicated Selection Bottleneck preprint. Each such item is flagged inline.

1. **Variance compression in the blend objective.** A synthesis aggregator "compresses all candidates into one blended response, forfeiting the advantage of having generated a standout candidate." The diversity gap, the gain a perfect selector extracts over a random one, is what a blending aggregator discards. Selection exploits variance; synthesis wastes it [single un-replicated preprint, 0 citations, single author, N=210/42 tasks, abstract+intro only, methods/results not in corpus] (Selection Bottleneck).

2. **Context distraction and semantic overlap at the aggregator.** Stuffing many overlapping responses into the aggregator's context makes keys semantically collide and the model loses signal, so *more* candidates can reduce the effective diversity the aggregator can act on. This is the explicit motivation for RMoA de-duplicating by embedding before aggregating (RMoA, https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.342/).

3. **Identity bias and correlation collapse.** LLMs preferentially agree with their own prior outputs, raising inter-agent correlation and shrinking the effective sample size of any rule that assumes independence; same-family models correlate more than cross-family, so a diverse-looking pool can be statistically near-homogeneous (Selection Bottleneck, citing prior work). The deepening pass surfaces a directly relevant measurement paper: representational coupling induced by communication between agents ("Representational Collapse in Multi-Agent LLM Committees", https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03809 [unverified: primary text not in corpus, surfaced only as a deepening-answer citation]).

4. **Sycophancy and majority-following at the aggregator.** The peer-reviewed Science Advances result covers only majority-following on neutral two-option choices. The stronger claims here, that models correct alone switched to wrong after seeing a group choose it (Asch-style), and that adversarial agents installed a misaligned state that persisted after removal, come from non-peer-reviewed follow-up preprints reported via a news aggregator ([via news], Superpower Daily aggregator). Any aggregation step that lets agents observe each other before combining plausibly risks manufacturing false consensus that erases the minority-correct view. Deepening-pass sources reinforce this as a distinct, alignment-resistant failure ("Not Just RLHF: Why Alignment Alone Won't Fix Multi-Agent Sycophancy", https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12991; "Too Polite to Disagree", https://aclanthology.org/2026.sigdial-1.56/ [both unverified: primary text not in corpus]).

A separate deepening question asks whether the flattening lives in the model or the objective, that is, whether the same aggregator LLM prompted to preserve and attribute dissent rather than summarize would stop homogenizing. The corpus does not settle it. One signal leans toward "partly the objective": homogenization is reported to be task dependent rather than intrinsic ("LLM Output Homogenization is Task Dependent", https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21267 [unverified: primary text not in corpus]). Council Mode's structured agreement/disagreement/unique-findings prompt is *not* usable as directional evidence here, because the ablation that would isolate the prompt from simply using a stronger synthesizer was not run. Against a pure prompt fix, one deepening source traces homogeneity back to shared base models, implying a representational floor no prompt can lift ("Is Convergence Inevitable? Tracing Output Homogeneity Back to Base Models", https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11426 [unverified: primary text not in corpus]).

## 3. Variance and minority preserving aggregation

The corpus's strongest theoretical contribution to *not* averaging away dissent is Hot PATE. It formalizes a "diversity-preserving ensemble sampler" and proves you can transfer the diversity of teacher distributions to the output without extra privacy cost, directly attacking the tension that raising agreement by suppressing diversity distorts the model and reduces utility. Hot PATE is the cleanest statement in the corpus that agreement-maximizing aggregation is actively harmful when the desired output is "a sample from a distribution" rather than a point, and it runs as a drop-in replacement for the diversity-collapsing sampler.

Maintaining Transient Diversity supplies the governing principle from collective-problem-solving science: *any* mechanism that extends the transient diversity of solutions, by widening the search space or slowing the diffusion of information and delaying consensus, improves the quality of the solution the group eventually converges on, across multi-armed bandits, NK landscapes, cumulative-innovation, and evolutionary-transmission models. The diversity must be transient so it does not persist long enough to block convergence. The trade-off is explicit: more transient diversity means higher final quality but longer time to converge. The named exceptions are trivially simple problems solvable by trial and error, or misaligned team incentives. The design implication for a synthesizer is counterintuitive: premature aggregation is the enemy; keep the pool spread out until late. A deepening source names the price directly: an epistemic landscape can make diversity of practice "better than best" but at a speed cost ("Better than Best: Epistemic Landscapes and Diversity of Practice", https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy-of-science/article/1125693C6EFCD39D13E3108840B87305 [unverified: primary text not in corpus]).

The forecasting and committee evidence agrees. Consensus forecasts are provably inefficient, over-weighting old public information and under-weighting new private information when forecasters have different information sets, and this is not due to individual irrationality; a simple adjustment cut errors 5% (IMF Consensus Forecasts). This is the wisdom-of-crowds failure mode in one line: naive averaging discards the idiosyncratic private signal that made diversity valuable. Diverse Views in Monetary Policy supplies the fix template: central banks that force a unified front suffer groupthink (2 dissents in 174 FOMC votes across 2021-22, versus common 6-3 splits at the UK MPC), and the recommended remedy is the judicial model, publish the majority ruling *with* concurring and dissenting opinions, never collapse to one voice. That is a concrete minority-preserving aggregation template: a synthesized output that carries its own dissent. No LLM paper in the readable corpus has adopted it, which is itself an opening.

Two sources mark the weighting side and its dual failure modes. SpecEM does not assume equal votes; it uses multiplicative-weight online updates so stronger models earn more influence during verification, segment by segment ("SpecEM", NeurIPS 2025, https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/4bd2ab7c614bc9ce646ba5559bb1fa67-Paper-Conference.pdf). A distant cross-domain analogy points the same direction: FedA4 is an image-classification federated-learning method (CIFAR/STL/Fashion-MNIST) whose "anti-bias aggregation" down-weights biased clients so a dominant-but-skewed source cannot capture the aggregate ("FedA4" [via news, image-classification federated learning; cross-domain analogy only], https://www.marktechpost.com/). Its detection signal, "low prediction-entropy model collapse," is over an image classifier's softmax and has no defined analogue for text perspectives, so FedA4 stands only as a loose analogy, not a transferable rule. Read alongside SpecEM it illustrates the two failure modes of weighting: too-equal loses quality (SpecEM's target), too-unequal lets a majority erase minorities (FedA4's target, by analogy). The open question of where the crossover between "down-weight the biased source" and "preserve the minority source" sits, and what signal distinguishes a *wrong* minority from a *valuable* one at aggregation time, is unresolved in the corpus (deepening consequence answer). The large-scale empirical anchor is a 21-method by 40-dataset comparison finding that aggregation-rule choice produces statistically significant performance differences (omnibus Friedman test), with boosting-style methods generally lagging and social-choice / collective-intelligence rules often beating plain majority vote ("Aggregation models in ensemble learning", https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1566253522001476). How you combine is a first-order lever, not a detail.

## 4. Synthesizer / aggregator design

The corpus converges on one design claim: the aggregator prompt and role determine the outcome more than the proposer set does.

Who synthesizes and how. Council Mode replaces "summarize these" with structured synthesis that must surface agreement, disagreement, and unique findings. Separately, and at a lower evidence tier, Grok 4.20 is a vendor-described, unbenchmarked product architecture [via news]: its Captain reportedly "aggregates the strongest elements, resolves remaining conflicts" with a dedicated Creative/Balance agent tasked with blind-spot and missing-perspective detection ("Grok 4.20 agents" [via news, vendor marketing narrative, no benchmark], https://www.nextbigfuture.com/). Keep Grok separate from the peer-reviewed Plurals and RMoA below. Plurals is the richest design vocabulary: persona'd Agents deliberate inside customizable Structures, and Moderators aggregate under user-set "combination instructions" that literally control how an agent folds in others' views; its thesis is interactional pluralism where the aggregation protocol itself is a diversity lever, not a "view from nowhere" ("Plurals", https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713675).

LLM-as-synthesizer biases are documented and matter. The Selection Bottleneck paper cites verbosity, position, self-enhancement, and sycophancy biases in LLM judges, and mitigates with a multi-judge panel using randomized candidate order and enforced agent-judge separation. Deepening sources corroborate position bias as systematic ("Judging the Judges: Position Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge", https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791 [unverified: primary text not in corpus]). Council Mode's own central unproven premise, flagged by its reviewer, is that the consensus model detects agreement and disagreement "without introducing new hallucinations or biases of its own": the synthesizer can become a new single point of failure and homogenization.

Hierarchical versus flat, weighting versus preserving. RMoA argues for hierarchical aggregation *with residual connections*: it drops the judge and uses Greedy Diversity Embedding Selection (embed each response, greedily pick the most dissimilar by vector similarity) to guarantee heterogeneous inputs, adds a Residual Extraction Agent that captures inter-layer *differences* so incremental information is not lost across deep stacks, and terminates adaptively on residual convergence, reporting state-of-the-art at lower cost. Rethinking MoA's Self-MoA-Seq instead does sliding-window sequential aggregation. RMoA and the Selection Bottleneck make diametrically opposite bets: RMoA deletes the judge and selects by embedding-diversity; the Selection Bottleneck says the judge is nearly everything. Both claim state-of-the-art, and the corpus does not adjudicate. A caution on RMoA specifically, raised by the deepening pass: embedding-diversity selection may preserve only surface or lexical variation, since two lenses can be embedding-close yet argumentatively opposite; stance-aware rather than plain semantic embeddings may be needed ("Perspectivized Stance Vectors", https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.83.pdf; "How Far Can We Extract Diverse Perspectives from LLMs?", https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09799 [both unverified: primary text not in corpus]).

A loose, off-topic anecdote, explicitly not about aggregation: Nvidia reports a custom harness (memory management plus a supervisor component) lifted an Opus-class model from 30% to 100% on ARC-AGI-3 [via news] ("Nvidia harness", Top Tech News, https://www.toptechnews.com/). This is a single-agent, sequential game-playing harness, not an aggregator over a candidate pool, so it does not bear on synthesis, selection, or combining perspectives. At most it is a loose "scaffolding can dominate model choice" anecdote, and it is not evidence for the combiner-is-the-lever reframing. The genuine version of that reframing rests on the sources above: if the aggregation layer carries much of the gain, an over-strong synthesizer becomes the new single point of homogenization (Council Mode's unproven premise). No source claims one aggregator design dominates across task types.

## 5. Evaluation

The corpus is quality-heavy and diversity-light, which is itself a finding: teams that measure only mean quality cannot detect flattening, because homogenization can *raise* a single-axis score while collapsing coverage. Note before reading: several sub-claims in this section rest only on titles and abstracts surfaced during deepening, not on retrieved primary text; each such item is flagged inline.

Quality metrics dominate: AlpacaEval 2.0, MT-Bench, Arena-Hard, TruthfulQA, HaluEval, and win-rate versus a baseline. The one repeatedly used *diversity* metric is the Vendi Score (Rethinking MoA), which let the authors plot a quality-diversity Pareto front and locate the optimum, the single clearest example in the corpus of co-reporting both axes. The Selection Bottleneck operationalizes *exploitable* diversity as the oracle-minus-mean gap and inter-model correlation, arguably better synthesis-specific metrics because they predict how much a combiner could gain rather than just how spread the pool is. Council Mode introduces a "bias variance" measure and a factual-consistency-versus-neutrality scatter. AI Assisted Scenario Building uses Scenario Diversity Analysis (pairwise distances, "does the set span the space of possibilities?") and found an LLM reproduced an analytical diversity ranking of SSP scenarios, correctly identifying that SSP2 and SSP4 are close and that SSP1 and SSP5 are close, a rare direct test of an LLM's ability to *measure* set coverage ("AI Assisted Scenario Building", UN/SDGs, https://sdgs.un.org/sites/default/files/2024-05/Carlsen_AI%20Assisted%20Scenario%20Building%20for%20Sustainable%20Development.pdf).

The coverage-retention question, whether an aggregator can be scored on the fraction of distinct source viewpoints traceable in the final output the way the Selection Bottleneck preprint scores the oracle-mean gap for quality, is the missing benchmark. A blocked idea-generation diversity paper may be relevant here but was not retrievable ("An Empirical Evaluation of Diversity in Multi-Agent Idea Generation", OpenReview, https://openreview.net/pdf/8517f920ec1ba2dcf15f88a674ae18ba34f56c2b.pdf [unverified: fetch returned only 495 chars, primary text not in corpus]). The deepening pass points at candidate building blocks from multi-document summarization: claim-level coverage of diverse factual information, claim-anchored attribution, and perspective-coverage benchmarks ("Beyond Factual Accuracy: Evaluating Coverage of Diverse Factual Information", https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.693.pdf; "Embrace Divergence for Richer Insights", https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09369 [both unverified: primary text not in corpus]). No single source in the readable corpus measures both quality gain and coverage retention of one synthesized output on the same axes; the metrics exist separately but are rarely reported together. To detect flattening in practice: measure the pool's spread before aggregation (Vendi Score or pairwise-distance span) and the retained spread after, and treat any large drop that is not accompanied by a quality gain as homogenization.

## 6. A synthesis protocol

A concrete, ordered recipe for a variance-preserving synthesizer stage. Distinguish sharply between generating lenses in isolation (upstream, not this stage) and combining them without flattening (this stage). Each rule below is tagged with its evidence tier: [well-supported], [single-preprint], [cross-domain-analogy], or [unread-title]. The best-supported single change is to avoid naive mean-blending of a quality-mixed pool; "select before you blend" is a hypothesis for recoverable-answer tasks, not the default (see rule 2).

Upstream (generate lenses in isolation, stated only to bound this stage):
- Generate each lens strictly independently [well-supported for the neutral-choice core; the stronger persistence claim rests on non-peer-reviewed follow-up preprints via a news aggregator]. Do not let lenses observe each other before the combine step, because agents that see each other's choices follow the majority on neutral choices, and non-peer-reviewed follow-ups suggest this can install a persistent wrong consensus ([via news] Superpower Daily aggregator; corroborated by the sycophancy-propagation deepening sources [unread-title]). Independence protects the private signal that averaging later discards (IMF Consensus Forecasts [well-supported]).
- Deliberately over-generate and keep the pool spread out; delay convergence (Transient Diversity [well-supported]).

This stage (combine without flattening), Do:
1. De-duplicate before aggregating [well-supported]. Embed candidates and drop near-duplicates so the aggregator's context is not flooded with semantically overlapping responses (RMoA). Consider stance-aware embeddings over plain semantic ones so argumentatively opposite lenses are not merged as "similar" (Perspectivized Stance Vectors [unread-title]).
2. Do not naively mean-blend a quality-mixed pool; treat "select over blend" as a hypothesis for recoverable-answer tasks, untested and possibly inverted for open-ended lens-synthesis [single-preprint]. In single-round generate-then-select pipelines *with a recoverable best answer and a competent judge*, one un-replicated preprint reports a judge-based selector beating MoA synthesis by +0.631 win rate with blending losing 42 of 42 tasks [single un-replicated preprint, 0 citations, single author, N=210/42 tasks, abstract+intro only, methods/results not in corpus] (Selection Bottleneck). For open-ended lens-synthesis (this skill's use case) the evidence is absent and may invert, and selection cannot exceed the best single candidate, so do not adopt selection-as-default there. Where selection is tried, selector quality plausibly matters, but s* is a proposed linear-model threshold, not yet operationalized or measured, so treat it as a direction to test, not a knob to read off.
3. When you must emit one combined artifact rather than pick one, use a dissent-carrying synthesis, not "summarize these" [well-supported]. This is the primary path for open-ended lens-synthesis, where the value is an emergent combined answer no single candidate held. The prompt must output the majority view plus explicitly labeled minority and dissenting views and unique findings, the judicial "majority opinion with published dissents" template (Diverse Views in Monetary Policy) operationalized as Council Mode's agreement/disagreement/unique-findings structure.
4. Weight without erasing [SpecEM well-supported; cap-the-dominant rule is cross-domain-analogy]. Give stronger sources more influence (SpecEM). By analogy from image-classification federated learning, consider capping influence so a dominant-but-skewed source cannot capture the aggregate (FedA4 [cross-domain-analogy]); note its "low prediction entropy" collapse signal has no defined text-perspective analogue, so this is a direction, not a prescription. Preserve minority sources unless there is a signal that the minority is *wrong* rather than merely rare.
5. Use residual/difference extraction in any deep or multi-round aggregation so incremental content is not lost across layers (RMoA [well-supported]).
6. Consider a diversity-preserving sampler when the desired output is a distribution rather than a single answer (creative sets, scenario sets), transferring the spread of perspectives into the output instead of collapsing to the mode (Hot PATE [well-supported for the private-distribution setting; drop-in transfer to non-private lens-synthesis is asserted, not shown]).

This stage, Avoid:
- Blending a quality-mixed pool to a mean; it drags the output down toward the weaker members (Rethinking MoA).
- A single over-strong synthesizer with no bias controls; it becomes the new single point of homogenization and failure (Council Mode's unproven premise), and inherits position, verbosity, self-preference, and sycophancy biases (Selection Bottleneck).
- Letting lenses see each other before combining ([via news] Superpower Daily aggregator; peer-reviewed core covers neutral choices only).
- Reporting only a mean-quality scalar; it cannot see flattening (Section 5).

This stage, Measure:
- Co-report a quality score (win-rate) and a coverage score (Vendi Score, pairwise-distance span, or the oracle-mean gap), never one alone (Rethinking MoA; Selection Bottleneck; Scenario Diversity Analysis).
- Compare pool spread before versus after aggregation; a large drop without a quality gain flags homogenization.
- Track inter-lens correlation; high same-family correlation means "mixing different models" added little independent signal (Selection Bottleneck).

Task-shape switch: *selection* is a hypothesis to test only for tasks with a recoverable best answer and a competent selector, and even there it caps quality at the best single candidate. For open-ended analytical or creative lens-combination, where no single candidate holds the whole answer and the output should be a faithful span of viewpoints, use *dissent-carrying synthesis* or a *diversity-preserving sampler*; the one preprint behind "select > blend" did not test this regime and may invert in it. This maps to the open question below.

## Open Questions

Drawn from the gap-bucket and consequence-bucket deepening answers.

- For a single synthesized output over analytical or creative lenses (not factual QA with one right answer), what is the analogue of "selector quality"? When quality is multi-dimensional and there is no oracle, does selection-beats-synthesis still hold, or does it flip back toward structured synthesis? The Selection Bottleneck result [single un-replicated preprint, abstract+intro only] was measured on tasks with recoverable best answers; the lens-combination regime may sit on the other side of the crossover (deepening gap answer 06). This scope condition is now stated in the bottom line, not only here.
- Can an aggregator be scored on a coverage-retention metric, the fraction of distinct source viewpoints traceable in the final output, the way the Selection Bottleneck scores the oracle-mean gap for quality? What is that metric for open-ended text? Candidate building blocks exist in multi-document summarization but have not been ported to lens-synthesis (deepening gap answer 07).
- Does embedding-diversity selection preserve *semantic* viewpoint diversity or only surface/lexical variation? Two lenses can be embedding-close yet argumentatively opposite, which would let RMoA-style selection silently drop genuine dissent (deepening gap answer 08).
- Where is the crossover between "down-weight the biased source" (FedA4, SpecEM) and "preserve the minority source" (Hot PATE, monetary policy)? What signal distinguishes a *wrong* minority from a *valuable* one at aggregation time (deepening consequence answer 04)?
- If teams adopt selection-over-synthesis, do they lose the genuine gains of blending, a combined answer no single candidate contained? Is there a task class where the combined-novel answer beats every individual one, making synthesis necessary despite its flattening cost? Deepening sources on answer-synthesis and "making, not taking, the best of N" suggest such a class exists but do not bound it (deepening consequence answer 03).
- If a few adversarial or sycophantic agents install a persistent wrong consensus, should divergent generation be kept strictly independent until the aggregation step in every pipeline, and what is the cost of that isolation (deepening consequence answer 05)?
- Is homogenization a prompt artifact or a representational one? If it traces to shared base models, no aggregator prompt can fully fix it (deepening root-cause answer 02).
- What is the quality/latency price of minority-preserving aggregation, and when is a decisive single answer worth more than a faithful-but-hedged one (Transient Diversity's explicit speed-accuracy trade-off; deepening consequence answer 04)?
