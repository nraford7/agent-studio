# Constructing Effective Agent Personas: Which Axis Drives Diversity: Research Bible (Focused Follow-Up)

## Executive Summary

Bottom line: on the current evidence, the persona's **functional stance**, what it is told to optimize and under what operating pressure, is the ONLY construction axis with a clean causal decomposition in this corpus. It is NOT shown to out-rank the other axes (occupation, demographics, name, CV, trait vectors, backstory), because no study in the corpus compares them against each other. The single cleanest causal result is a 2x2 factorial that varied only two stance components (safety-first vs lean-efficiency, high vs low time pressure) and moved reasoning breadth, test selection, and safety behavior in a suggestive direction within one model, including a trap-case failure that appeared in exactly one stance cell and nowhere else ("Decomposing Persona Prompts for Simulated Clinical Reasoning," Cureus 2026, n=224 runs on one un-versioned model (GPT-5.2 Pro) via chat-UI defaults, 28 synthetic Japanese vignettes, trap result = 8 events total; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13107492/). The corroboration is thin in primary-text terms: one direct decomposition (Cureus) plus indirect signals; the nearest corroborating papers were retrieved only as titles and are unverified. No study in this corpus runs a clean head-to-head factorial across all six construction axes on the same task, so "stance is the only clean causal evidence, not a proven ranking" is the honest statement, not "stance wins."

Key findings:

- Task-relevant expert roles reliably drive variability but not accuracy. Across 162 personas, 7 LLMs, 5 datasets, and ~90M generations, role personas produced more variability than controls in every scenario (they DO drive diversity), yet "expert personas may not be the best performer and demographic personas outperform the no-persona baseline in some scenarios," with a top-to-bottom accuracy gap of 38.56 percentage points that was not organized around expertise ("Helpful assistant or fruitful facilitator?", PLOS One, https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0325664). The unreliability is for correctness, not for diversity.
- Sociodemographic labels are a weak driver of *accuracy* on average (they do not reliably predict the human answer). Persona variables explain under 10% of variance in human annotations for most subjective NLP tasks, and persona prompting only helps in proportion to that explanatory power ("Quantifying the Persona Effect," anonymous/unrefereed ACL submission (unrefereed), corpus stub, abstract-level).
- How you phrase a demographic persona matters as much as which one you pick. Interview-style Q&A format plus name-based (implicit) priming reduce stereotyping and improve alignment; explicit and structured demographic descriptors trigger stereotype effects ("The Prompt Makes the Person(a)," EMNLP Findings 2025, https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1261).
- Free-text narrative backstories on **base** (not instruction-tuned) models bind personas better than structured trait or demographic labels, reproducing Pew opinion distributions with smaller human-model gaps and higher internal consistency ("Binding LLMs to Virtual Personas" / Anthology, https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.1110).
- Constructed personas caricature: GPT-4 emulations of 400 real people had convergent validity of 0.90-0.94 but factor loadings of 0.87-0.95 versus 0.52-0.65 in the humans, too internally consistent to be a real person ("Evaluating the ability of LLMs to emulate personality," Scientific Reports, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-84109-5).
- Personas drift: when instructed to hold a persona, LLMs show low rank-order value stability that degrades further with conversation length ("Stick to your role!", PLOS One, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0309114).
- Personalized models fabricate: every one of 12 models over-inferred 35-49% of user attributes (mean 41.6%) [via news; unverified primary], and self-reported fabrication correlated inversely with judged fabrication (rho = -0.60) [via news; unverified primary] ("MirageBench" / The Personalization Mirage, AI Weekly writeup).

What to actually do: build the persona from a **functional stance** (optimization target + operating pressure + one adversarial trap it must not fail; the "trap" is a heuristic extrapolated from the clinical trap design, untested outside clinical triage), add identity through **implicit cues** (a name, a short first-person narrative, one internal contradiction) rather than explicit demographic labels, keep demographic conditioning **off by default** and opt-in only for demonstrably demographically-loaded tasks, and for a diverse debating ensemble vary the **stance and values** across members rather than their job titles. Caveat on the ensemble recommendation: no study in this corpus directly validates stance-diverse ensembles for productive reasoning diversity; this is a grounded design inference, not a measured result.

## 1. The construction axes, compared

The six candidate axes are: (1) role / expertise / occupation; (2) sociodemographic identity; (3) epistemic stance / cognitive style / values; (4) named real-person exemplar; (5) Big Five / psychometric trait vectors; (6) psychographic / worldview / backstory / quirks. No study in this corpus crosses all six in one factorial on one task, so the comparison below assembles the best direct and indirect evidence per axis. Only one study offers a clean causal decomposition (the Cureus stance 2x2); it does not compare stance against the other axes.

**Role / expertise (Axis 1) reliably drives variability/diversity but is an unreliable lever on correctness.** The largest single study, "Helpful assistant or fruitful facilitator?" (PLOS One), assigned 162 personas across 12 categories to 7 LLMs on 5 datasets, generating ~90M outputs, and controlled prompt-sensitivity with 30 paraphrases of "a helpful assistant." Personas produced greater variability than the control set in every scenario, so role DOES drive diversity; what it does not reliably drive is accuracy. The accuracy spread was 38.56 percentage points between the best and worst persona; the spread was not cleanly organized by expertise, and "expert personas may not be the best performer" while "demographic personas outperform the no persona baseline in some scenarios" (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0325664). The paper also reports a bias-accuracy trade-off: assigning the in-group persona reduces bias against that group but lowers QA accuracy. The corpus is not one-sided here: PLOS One's own related-work foregrounds conflicting evidence that role and demographic personas sometimes DO help (it cites results where personas improve trustworthiness and domain-specific accuracy, and its own finding that demographic personas beat the no-persona baseline in some scenarios), so "role is unreliable" is a statement about central tendency, not a claim that role never helps. Where role does pay off is as a **multiplier**: "Open models, closed minds?" (AAAI, 12 open LLMs, MBTI + BFI) found most models retain their intrinsic personality when instructed otherwise ("closed-minded"), but "combining role and personality conditioning can enhance the agents' ability to mimic human personalities" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07115). Several role-effect papers cited in the deepening round, "When A Helpful Assistant Is Not Really Helpful" (EMNLP Findings 2024), "Persona is a Double-Edged Sword" (IJCNLP 2025), and "Persona-Assigned LLMs Exhibit Human-Like Motivated Reasoning" (ACL Findings 2026), point the same direction (role personas add variance and sometimes bias, rarely reliable accuracy) but their primary texts were not retrieved into this corpus [unverified: primary text not in corpus].

**The one clean causal decomposition in the corpus isolates stance, not identity, and finds stance moves reasoning within a single model.** The clinical 2x2 ("Decomposing Persona Prompts," Cureus 2026) ran n=224 runs on one un-versioned model (GPT-5.2 Pro) via consumer chat-UI defaults, with no temperature or model-version control, over 28 synthetic Japanese-language emergency-department vignettes. It did not vary occupation, demographics, exemplar, trait vector, or backstory; it crossed time-pressure framing (high/low) with optimization target (safety-first/lean-efficiency). Within this one model, both components moved reasoning significantly with no detected interaction (interaction test is underpowered at this n): safety-first raised proposed tests (beta = 1.32, p < 0.001) and diagnostic breadth measured as Shannon entropy across the top-five differential (beta = 0.247, p < 0.001); high time-pressure narrowed both (beta = -1.05 and -0.246, p < 0.001). Safety-first also raised discharge safety-net specificity (mean 4.5 vs 2.6 on a 5-point scale). Contraindication or sequencing violations occurred **only** in the high-pressure/lean cell (8 of 56 runs, 14.3%), and in the four trap cases the violation rate was 8/8 under high/lean versus 0/24 across the other three cells, a total of 8 trap events (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13107492/). This is suggestive within one model that the stance content of a role, what it optimizes, under what pressure, drives both reasoning quality and output diversity (differential entropy); it is not a robust cross-model benchmark, and because the design contains no other axis it says nothing about whether stance out-ranks role, demographics, exemplar, traits, or backstory. Separately, the Anthology dissertation (below) compares backstory-conditioning against prior persona methods and finds narrative wins on Pew-ATP fidelity; that is a method comparison, not a stance-vs-identity factorial, and does not add a second causal decomposition of the same class.

**Sociodemographic identity (Axis 2) is the most studied and the most cautionary axis.** Its ceiling is quantified by "Quantifying the Persona Effect" (anonymous/unrefereed ACL submission, not a published Findings paper): sociodemographic variables explain under 10% of variance in human annotations for most subjective NLP datasets (unrefereed), persona prompting helps in a roughly linear relationship to that explanatory power, and where the demographic genuinely predicts the answer it can explain up to 81% of variance (unrefereed; rare high-signal subset) (corpus stub, abstract/line-level text; the published-anthology URL is not asserted). So demographics are a weak driver of *accuracy* on average and a strong predictor only on the minority of demographically-loaded tasks.

**Epistemic stance / values (Axis 3) is underexplored as an explicit axis but is implicitly the winner** (see the clinical 2x2). Its flip side is instability: "Stick to your role!" (PLOS One, Schwartz values via PVQ-40, 21 LLMs) shows value expression is highly context-dependent, and when a specific persona is instructed, rank-order stability is low and degrades further with conversation length (https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0309114). Stance is a potent driver you can move easily but that will not hold on its own.

**Named real-person exemplar (Axis 4) binds rank-order well but sands off the messiness.** The Scientific Reports emulation study role-played 400 real individuals from their Big Five profiles and got convergent validity of 0.90-0.94 (mean 0.91), impressively high rank-order agreement, but the emulated profiles were far too factorially pure (loadings 0.87-0.95 vs human 0.52-0.65) and had internal consistency of 0.97-0.99 vs human 0.79-0.89 (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-84109-5). "Stick to your role!" simulated named real-world personas (e.g., Gandalf) alongside fictional characters and found they share the same instability problem.

**Big Five / psychometric trait vectors (Axis 5) are the best-validated shaping axis, with a hard reliability caveat.** The Nature Machine Intelligence framework applied a full psychometric protocol to 18 LLMs and showed synthetic personality is measurable, reliable, valid, and shapeable along desired dimensions (104 trait adjectives at 9 levels), and that shaped personality carries into downstream tasks, but "evidence of reliability and validity ... is stronger for larger and instruction-fine-tuned models" (https://www.nature.com/articles/... Nature Machine Intelligence). "Open models, closed minds?" is the counterweight: on many open models trait requests do not take. The mechanistic version is persona vectors: linear directions in layer activations corresponding to traits (evil, sycophancy, hallucination) that can be added or subtracted to amplify or attenuate a trait, extracted from Qwen2.5-7B or Llama-3.1-8B; the news summary reports that subtraction at inference reduces both the trait and MMLU while addition during fine-tuning reduces the trait without degrading MMLU [via news] (https://... Persona Vectors DeepLearning.AI writeup). This is a construction axis that bypasses prompting entirely; note the primary Anthropic/UT-Austin/Berkeley paper text was not retrieved, only a news summary.

**Psychographic / backstory / quirks (Axis 6) is the strongest positive construction result in the corpus.** The Anthology framework (a UC Berkeley dissertation, Moon 2025; not journal peer-reviewed) generates first-person backstories by simple prompting ("Tell me about yourself") on **pretrained base models** and matches them to a target demographic distribution. It compares this backstory-conditioning against existing persona-conditioning methods and finds the narrative approach wins on Pew-ATP fidelity: LLMs "reproduce population-level opinion distributions from the Pew Research Center's American Trends Panel with smaller distributional shifts between human and model responses and higher internal consistency than existing persona-conditioning methods," reproduce partisan asymmetries in moral judgment, and produce human-aligned play in Dictator and Trust games (https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.1110). This is a method comparison, not a controlled factorial isolating narrative-vs-structured encoding on one task. Two structural claims: narrative coherence is essential (longer multi-turn backstories give "deep persona binding" vs "shallow imitation of social stereotypes"), and instruction-tuned chat models "override narrative cues with safety or normative alignment objectives," so base models are the better substrate. A craft-community heuristic (not evidence-based) echoes the coherence point: the Character Design Deep Sheet argues the liveliness of a persona comes from "the contradiction that drives them, the verbal tic ... and the truth they'd lie to a therapist about," not "dead data, hair color, favorite food, Zodiac sign," and asserts that smaller models flatten this into Hollywood archetypes; this is a paid-prompt marketing page with no measurement, so treat it as craft practice, not a finding.

Confidence-in-evidence ranking (how clean the evidence is, NOT how large the effect, and NOT a cross-axis effect-size ranking): (1) functional stance/priority content has the only clean causal decomposition, though it is single-study and single-model (clinical 2x2); (2) narrative backstory on base models has the strongest positive method-comparison result for simulating humans (Anthology); (3) psychometric trait shaping is reliable but only on large instruct models (Nature Machine Intelligence; AAAI); (4) sociodemographic labels are the most studied, weak on average for accuracy and prone to stereotype injection (Quantifying Persona Effect; Lutz). Occupational-title role and named-exemplar sit lower in evidence quality as standalone drivers and act mainly as multipliers. Note: the four headline effect sizes cited across these axes (Cureus betas on test count and Shannon entropy; PLOS One's 38.56-point accuracy spread; Quantifying's <10% and 81% variance) are on incommensurable scales, different tasks, models, and metrics, and cannot be ordered against one another; this list ranks confidence in the evidence, not magnitude of effect.

## 2. Which persona components carry the signal

**Components that move outputs in useful ways:** functional priorities (what to optimize, under what pressure), the only components in the corpus with a clean causal decomposition (clinical 2x2); narrative coherence and backstory length (Anthology); implicit identity cues such as names and conversation history (Lutz; Weeber); and internal contradiction / quirks for liveliness (craft-community heuristic, not evidence-based; Character Deep Sheet). "One Persona, Many Cues, Different Results" (Weeber et al., arXiv 2026, 6 persona cues x 7 LLMs x 4 tasks) found cues are highly correlated on average yet "produce substantial variance in responses across personas," and it cautions explicitly against drawing conclusions from a single cue (https://arxiv.org/... "One Persona, Many Cues" [publication_3cf59d545dc2 in corpus]).

**Components that add noise or inject bias:** explicit and structured demographic descriptors. Lutz crosses 3 role-adoption formats (direct "You are a Black woman" / third-person "Think of..." / interview Q&A) x 3 priming strategies (name-based "Ms. Gonzalez" / explicit "a Hispanic woman" / structured "a person of race Hispanic"). Interview format plus name-based priming produced "less stereotypical associations, better opinion alignment, and reduced disparities between groups," whereas explicit descriptors "may be more likely to trigger stereotypes associated with demographic groups, as they could be more directly tied to group-based characteristics in the training data" (https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1261). Weeber adds that "highly explicit (but potentially unnatural) cues lead to stronger personalization bias compared to more implicit cues."

**Does specificity help or inject bias?** Specificity that is narratively integrated helps; specificity that is listed and explicit mostly adds bias. The emulation study is instructive: adding demographic information (age set to 50, country set to Britain) *changed* convergent validities for certain traits, sometimes reducing them, even as it improved prediction of external criteria (Scientific Reports). So more specificity is not monotonically better; it is a trade between fidelity to a schema and fidelity to a real individual.

**The sociodemographic-cue sensitivity is the sharpest single warning for the user's skill.** The "same" persona expressed through different cues gives different results, and a surprising size effect appears: OLMo-2-7B outperformed Llama-3.3-70B at simulating demographic groups (Lutz), so bigger is not better for demographic construction. A news writeup reports that quantized Llama-70B produces near-zero same-trait correlations on personality mimicry [via news] (Chameleon LLMs writeup, https://... df478ac1b2cb). The practical implication: never validate a persona on one cue, and do not assume a frontier model constructs demographic personas more faithfully than a mid-size one.

## 3. The fidelity gap

Constructed personas fail fidelity in two opposite directions: they compress real diversity and they invent unsupported detail.

**Compression / caricature.** GPT-4 emulations of real people are too coherent to be real: factor loadings of 0.87-0.95 versus 0.52-0.65 in the humans they copied, internal consistency of 0.97-0.99 versus 0.79-0.89, and near-zero cross-loadings where the humans had genuine cross-loadings, "emulated personality scores were much more factorially pure than the self-reported personalities they were based on" (Scientific Reports). The robustness of role-play also "wanes as the complexity of the roles increases." A likely mechanism, flagged in the deepening round, is that the model generates from an idealized schema of a trait rather than sampling a real cross-loaded human; the CoMPosT caricature framework (EMNLP 2023) is cited on this point but its primary text was not retrieved [unverified: primary text not in corpus].

**Value and personality instability.** Constructed personas do not hold their values: instructed personas show low rank-order stability that "further diminishes with conversation length" (Stick to your role!). This directly contradicts the geometric "identity is a stable attractor" claim discussed in Section 4.

**Sanitization of generated personas.** When LLMs *generate* personas at scale, the output skews to "middle-aged, aspirational, and overwhelmingly positive (i.e., upbeat/optimistic) characters, while non-binary identities and many real-world occupations remain underrepresented", a "narrative sanitization that both flattens representational diversity and embeds normative assumptions" (40,000 personas, 4 languages, two open-weight generators; "All too perfect," Artificial Intelligence Review, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-026-11641-3).

**Fabrication.** Personalized models invent user attributes: MirageBench evaluated 12 models across 7 families on 143,616 judged claims [via news; unverified primary] and found every model over-infers 35-49% of user attributes (mean 41.6%) [via news; unverified primary], task-dependent from 27.0% (gift recommendation) [via news; unverified primary] to 57.8% (apartment description) [via news; unverified primary], with attributes accumulating 5-15 per turn and removal rates as low as 0.4% [via news; unverified primary] (The Personalization Mirage / MirageBench, AI Weekly writeup). The primary paper text was not retrieved; figures come from a news summary, so treat exact percentages as [confidence: medium].

The single dedicated "Persona Fidelity Gap" paper named in the prior corpus was an OpenReview browser-check stub and its full text is not retrievable here [unverified: primary text not in corpus], its specific compression figures cannot be quoted.

## 4. Representation and prompt structure

Encoding changes results as much as content does.

**Role-adoption format.** Interview Q&A beats direct "You are..." which beats third-person for reducing stereotyping and improving alignment (Lutz). This argues for a Q&A / dialogue scaffold over a single declarative system-prompt sentence.

**Priming channel.** Implicit name > explicit label > structured category, for alignment and low bias (Lutz; Weeber).

**Substrate.** Base model + backstory > instruct model + labels, because alignment tuning overrides narrative cues (Anthology). The dissertation's own phrase for why base models work is that their "heterogeneous 'mixture of voices' enable backstories to bind naturally through prefix conditioning," whereas instruction-tuned models "override narrative cues with safety or normative alignment objectives." This is the corpus's proposed answer to why explicit labels inject stereotypes while names and backstories do not: labels activate a small set of strongly-associated group tokens, whereas names and narratives distribute conditioning across many weak heterogeneous associations. The supporting papers in the deepening round ("Simulating Identity, Propagating Bias," EMNLP Findings 2025; "Reading Between the Prompts," EMNLP 2025) develop this but their primary texts were not retrieved into this corpus [unverified: primary text not in corpus].

**Trait vectors / activation steering.** A representation-level axis that bypasses prompting: persona vectors add or subtract a trait direction in activation space [via news]. This occupies a different substrate (weights/activations) from backstory prompting and, importantly, the two have never been compared head-to-head for diversity or reasoning quality, they are treated as separate literatures.

**The geometric attractor claim, treated with caution.** A single-author, non-peer-reviewed preprint ("Identity as Attractor," Vasilenko) reports that identity documents create attractor-like clusters in activation space (paraphrases converge tighter than controls, Cohen's d > 1.88, p < 10^-27 after Bonferroni, on Llama-3.1-8B, replicated on Gemma-2-9B), with structural completeness of the identity document needed to reach the basin [via news; disputed] (https://... bc8f3f6b172c). Flags: not peer-reviewed, unusually large effect sizes, only 8-9B models, no independent replication, and it sits in direct tension with the observed length-driven instability in "Stick to your role!". Treat as preliminary.

**Model- and language-dependence of the default persona.** Anthropic's analysis of 309,000+ real conversations found Claude's personality varies by model and by language along four dimensions (warmth/rigor, candor/execution, depth/brevity, deference/caution), and "researchers don't yet know why" [via news] (https://... 56a0dd9b1957). Any constructed persona rides on top of a shifting default, so cross-lingual and cross-model construction cannot be assumed stable.

## 5. Failure modes

- **Stereotype injection from explicit labels.** Explicit and structured demographic descriptors trigger stereotype effects, hitting non-binary, Hispanic, and Middle Eastern personas hardest (Lutz); explicit cues also produce stronger personalization bias (Weeber).
- **Persona / value collapse and drift.** Instructed personas show low rank-order stability that decays with conversation length (Stick to your role!), long-running agents lose their persona over time.
- **"Closed-minded" models.** Most open LLMs retain their intrinsic personality and ignore an imposed trait; only a few mirror it (AAAI). Trait construction fails silently on the wrong substrate.
- **Aspiration / positivity bias and sanitization.** Generated personas skew upbeat, middle-aged, aspirational, and under-represent non-binary identities and many occupations (All too perfect).
- **Over-inference / fabrication.** Personalized models invent 35-49% of user attributes, compounding ~linearly over turns [via news; unverified primary] (MirageBench).
- **Self-monitoring inversion.** The models that self-report the least fabrication are judged the worst (rho = -0.60, p = 0.044; Qwen3-8B self-rated 13.0% vs judged 48.7%), so model self-report is a broken safety comparator [via news; unverified primary] (MirageBench).
- **Over-coherence as a fidelity failure.** Emulated personas are more internally consistent than real humans (Scientific Reports), the failure is caricature, not noise.
- **Over-anthropomorphism / accountability gaps.** Anthropomorphic AI framing "creates gaps in accountability" (Brookings, institutional slice, context only).

## 6. A construction protocol

An ordered recipe for a single effective persona and for an effective diverse set. Each step is tied to a cited finding. Structure per step: Do / Bias-check / Test / Measure.

**Step 1: Lead with functional stance, not identity.**
Do: specify the persona as (a) an optimization target, (b) an operating pressure, and (c) one adversarial trap it must not fail (the trap is a heuristic extrapolated from the clinical trap design, untested outside clinical triage). Example: "Optimize for catching low-probability high-severity failures; you are under no time pressure; you must never approve the plan if step X was skipped."
Bias-check: confirm the stance does not smuggle in a demographic stereotype.
Test: run the persona against its adversarial trap case.
Measure: entropy / breadth of options generated, and trap-case pass rate.
Grounding: the clinical 2x2 suggests, within one un-versioned model (n=224 runs, chat-UI defaults, 28 synthetic Japanese vignettes, trap result = 8 events), that stance components (safety-first, time pressure) move testing, diagnostic breadth (entropy), and trap-case violations (Cureus 2026); this is suggestive within one model, not a robust cross-model result.

**Step 2: Add identity through implicit, narrative cues.**
Do: give a name and a short first-person backstory (a few sentences of "who I am and what shaped me") rather than a demographic label or an attribute checklist.
Bias-check: no explicit "you are a [race] [gender]" descriptor unless Step 5 justifies it.
Test: compare outputs under name-only vs explicit-label versions of the same persona.
Measure: stereotype-association rate and between-group disparity.
Grounding: interview format + name-based priming reduce stereotyping and improve alignment; explicit descriptors trigger stereotypes (Lutz); backstories bind deeper than labels (Anthology).

**Step 3: Prefer coherent narrative over attribute lists, and add one contradiction.**
Do: write the persona as connected prose with at least one internal contradiction and one verbal tic; longer, coherent backstories bind deeper.
Bias-check: reject clichés and "dead data" attribute lists.
Test: check whether the persona holds the contradiction or flattens to an archetype.
Measure: qualitative liveliness plus internal-consistency comparison to a real target (guard against over-purity).
Grounding: narrative coherence is essential for deep binding (Anthology). The contradiction/verbal-tic/hidden-secret advice is a craft-community heuristic, not evidence-based (Character Deep Sheet, a paid-prompt marketing page with no measurement). The only grounded part of this step is the over-purity finding: emulations that are too factorially pure are caricatures (Scientific Reports).

**Step 4: Choose the substrate deliberately.**
Do: for high-fidelity human simulation, prefer a pretrained base model + backstory; for a safe deployed agent, accept that an instruction-tuned model will partly override narrative and lean harder on Steps 1 and 5.
Bias-check: on instruct models, verify the assistant default has not collapsed the persona.
Test: probe whether the trait/stance actually took (many open models are "closed-minded").
Measure: trait-request take-rate; alignment gap to target distribution.
Grounding: instruction tuning overrides narrative cues, base models bind better (Anthology); trait shaping is reliable only on larger instruct models (Nature Machine Intelligence); many open models retain intrinsic traits (AAAI).

**Step 5: Gate demographic conditioning; default it OFF.**
Do: add explicit demographic cues only when the task is demonstrably demographically-loaded (the demographic predicts the human answer).
Bias-check: estimate whether the demographic plausibly explains meaningful variance for this task.
Test: run with and without the demographic cue.
Measure: alignment gain vs stereotype-injection cost; demographics explain under 10% of variance on most tasks.
Grounding: persona variables explain under 10% of annotation variance on most tasks, up to 81% where they matter (Quantifying Persona Effect, anonymous/unrefereed ACL submission); bias-accuracy trade-off from demographic personas (PLOS One).

**Step 6: Validate with multiple cues and external verification, never one.**
Do: express the persona through several cue formulations and average; tag any stored/inferred attribute by provenance (stated / inferred-with-evidence / generated-without-evidence).
Bias-check: never use model self-report as the safety comparator.
Test: cross-cue variance and multi-turn attribute-accumulation check.
Measure: response variance across cues; over-inference rate via external judge.
Grounding: single-cue conclusions are unsafe (Weeber); provenance tagging and external audit needed because models fabricate 35-49% and self-report is inversely correlated with truth (MirageBench [via news; unverified primary]).

**Step 7: Re-anchor over long runs.**
Do: for long-running agents, periodically re-inject the identity/stance rather than trusting a one-shot system prompt; consider distributing identity across memory/files [speculative; from a disputed preprint].
Bias-check: confirm re-anchoring does not accrete fabricated attributes.
Test: measure value/stance stability at increasing conversation lengths.
Measure: rank-order stability over turns.
Grounding: persona stability decays with conversation length (Stick to your role!); a distributed "identity anchors across files" approach is proposed in the geometric line [via news; disputed].

**For a diverse debating ensemble.**
Do: vary members along **stance and values** (what each optimizes, its operating pressure, its adversarial trap) rather than along job titles or demographics. Give each a name and a short backstory with one contradiction. Keep demographics off unless the debate topic is demographically-loaded.
Grounding: stance is the only axis with a clean causal decomposition in this corpus (Cureus 2026, single-study, single-model); it is not shown to out-rank the other axes, because no study compares them. Expert-title role is an unreliable driver of *accuracy* though it does drive variability (PLOS One); demographic labels are a weak *accuracy* driver on average and risk stereotype injection (Quantifying Persona Effect, anonymous/unrefereed; Lutz). Note: no study in this corpus directly validates stance-diverse ensembles for productive reasoning diversity, so this is a grounded design inference, not a measured result.

## Open Questions

- **No head-to-head across the six axes.** No study runs a clean factorial across role x demographic x stance x exemplar x trait-vector x backstory on the same tasks and models. The clinical 2x2 is the closest but crosses only two stance sub-components (Cureus 2026).
- **Backstory-on-base vs trait-vector-on-instruct has never been compared** for either diversity or reasoning quality; they occupy different substrates and separate literatures (Anthology; persona vectors [via news]).
- **Reasoning-quality and productive-diversity are under-instrumented.** Most work measures alignment-to-humans or bias, not reasoning quality; the clinical entropy metric is a rare exception.
- **Does functional-stance dominance generalize beyond clinical triage** to open-ended reasoning and debating ensembles? Suggestive supporting papers (motivated reasoning, "double-edged sword") were not retrieved into this corpus [unverified: primary text not in corpus].
- **Can instruction-tuned agents get backstory-level binding,** or does alignment tuning make deep narrative binding impossible without a base model (Anthology)?
- **Do geometric attractor effects, if real, predict drift resistance**, reconciling the attractor claim (Cohen's d > 1.88) with observed length-driven instability [disputed; via news] vs (Stick to your role!)?
- **Cross-lingual construction is barely studied for reasoning effects,** despite personality shifting substantially by language (Claude 309k [via news]).
- **Minimal effective persona.** Where is the point past which more specificity only adds bias, is name + one-line stance + one contradiction the floor? No study in the corpus pins this down.
